Simulation 4

Special Test Roulette: Lower Extremity

Instructor Simulation Summary

Students will research and serve as a patient and proctor of a specific injury requiring a special test. As a health care provided (HCP), students will participate at 5 stations by completing special tests and answering patient or proctor questions at each station.

Instructor-Only Information

If you are going to use the suggested format of six stations, be sure to assign Presimulation Activity 5 Special Test Selection to students before the simulation. Each student should receive the “Information for Station” for the station for which they will be serving as the patient and proctor.

Scenario Synopsis

Six total stations will be set up for this simulation. HCPs will be divided into groups of two and assigned one station for which they will be a patient and proctor. During the first portion of the simulation, HCP 1 will be the patient, and then during the second portion of the simulation, HCP 2 will be the patient. The situation will not change, just the roles will switch. The pair will be assigned an injury and must learn and know the relevant special tests for this injury, then take turns acting as the patient and proctor during their rotation as the patient. Student HCPs will rotate through the remaining five stations for which they are not assigned as the patient or proctor. Each station will have an examination table. No special tools or equipment will be needed for the students to complete these simulations.

Content Area

Students must be familiar with selecting and using special tests for lower-extremity orthopedic examination and diagnosis.

Learning Objectives

Identify special tests appropriate to assess a differential diagnosis

Correctly perform each chosen special test

Interpret the results of each special test to correctly diagnose, differentially diagnose, or refer for specific testing required to diagnose the injury

CAATE 2020 Standards

Standard 71

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

This simulation involves multiple scenarios with brief opportunities for clinical decision making. As such, the Diamond debriefing method may be useful to articulate and address any clinical and application-based questions.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

This simulation could be modified to include any special tests students learn in their orthopedic evaluation courses. This simulation could also be used as a clinical education readiness tool or to practice for a clinical rotation prior to the students’ beginning the rotation. You may also choose to have students work in pairs and alternate who is the HCP and patient, and then each complete three of the prescribed stations.

This roulette simulation has been coordinated with various providers previously. Using actual providers from a particular setting or a variety of settings is very informative for students. For instance, we have used clinical personnel and field management athletic trainers (ATs), and each person performs tests differently based on time-lapse, clinical rules, location, hand size, and so on. With this format, students quickly rotate through multiple stations and do not have to be a patient or a proctor. If you use these clinicians as the patient and proctor, you may consider having a short debrief immediately after the simulation to allow these providers to discuss both their differences and logic and their work settings.

If you would like to add additional stations to this simulation schedule or substitute for different injuries, you may choose to select the following scenarios:

Optional Station 7 Information

A player who was at the bottom of a rugby pileup complains of foot pain and can barely walk to the sideline. The player reports sharp pain in the foot and difficulty weight bearing. There is no deformity. What special test or prediction tool will you use to evaluate the severity of this patient’s foot pain?

Answer: Ottawa ankle rules (fracture with sharp, pinpoint pain at the base of fifth metatarsal and inability to weight bear for four steps). Long bone compression, tuning fork, pinpoint tenderness. Lisfranc tests (patient has no movement with piano key test, no bruising on the bottom of the foot, no pain in the second metatarsal region, squeeze test)

Diagnosis: Metatarsal fracture (base of fifth metatarsal)

Optional Station 8 Information

During a basketball game, a player lands awkwardly after a jump. She experiences immediate pain and cannot walk. What tests will you perform to differentially diagnose this injury?

Answer: Lisfranc tests: (piano key for second metatarsal, single-leg heel rise, discoloration on the bottom of the foot, squeeze test). Ottawa ankle rules negative (fracture with sharp, pinpoint pain at the base of fifth metatarsal and inability to weight bear for four steps). Long bone compression, tuning fork, pinpoint tenderness

Diagnosis: Lisfranc injury
