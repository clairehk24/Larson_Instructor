Simulation 9

Coach Education

Instructor Simulation Summary

Health care providers (HCPs) will schedule a meeting with a coach to discuss a medical condition for an athlete. There are three possible medical conditions to discuss: sickle cell trait, asthma, and epilepsy. Students will be informed within 24 hours of the meeting which condition they will be addressing. Students should prepare for all three.

Instructor-Only Summary

Be sure students prepare for all three medical conditions, and inform students 24 hours before the simulation which condition they will be addressing. The proctor for this simulation is usually also the coach. This format is important for the student because the coach’s perspective in this situation is important. However, we have also proctored the simulation along with the coach. To accomplish this, we have used a telehealth platform to record the session or have watched with a blank or dark screen, silently observed during the interaction. The method we have chosen largely depends on our familiarity with the embedded personnel (EP) playing the coach role, as well as the amount and type of feedback or perspectives that may be useful for the student. This simulation has been scheduled in pairs but can be done individually. Finally, the athletes are gender-neutral in the descriptions. To make the discussion easier, please specify gender preferences in advance for the students and the coach.

Scenario Synopsis

The purpose of this simulation is to discuss a medical condition with a coach by explaining how this condition affects the athlete, the team, the coach, and the HCP or HCP team.

Content Area

Familiarity with sickle cell trait, epilepsy, and asthma is helpful. Ability to consider how these conditions affect a player’s practice, competition, training, and psychology is important.

Learning Objectives

  1. Appropriately present clinical, evidence-based information to a coach about a specific medical condition (asthma, sickle cell trait, epilepsy), including its presentation and emergent management
  2. Discuss how this will affect the athlete: psychologically, physically, and daily management (nutrition, preparation, rest breaks, etc.), including the athlete’s ability to practice and compete
  3. Professionally consider and respond to questions or complications presented by the coach and arrive at a consensus on steps moving forward
  4. Discuss how prevention and emergent management of the specific condition may affect or change the status quo (need new equipment, need to have a physician available, a specific management plan, etc.)
  5. Discuss the role of the athlete, coach, athletic trainers (ATs), team, or others in relation to this condition and the athlete

HCP Prebrief

You will meet with the coach at your predetermined time and format. However, if the coach needs to reschedule, please work with the coach to reschedule by the deadline of the assignment.

The interaction may be recorded. This simulation is scheduled for up to 15 minutes.

A few important things to remember for this simulation. All the details about your patient have not been given to you. You will need to fill in or make up some of the details. If there is information you do not know but need it to successfully complete or discuss the simulation, you can make up this information. For instance, if the coach asks how long the player has been doing the sport, you can say, “I don’t know” because you perceive it not to be relevant, or you can say, “five years.” Whatever your answer, play that answer out. You cannot simply say, “I don’t know,” unless you take responsibility for not knowing. For example, if asked about the PPE (preparticipation exam) and you don’t have information from that exam, you must take responsibility for that and explain when you will obtain it, or you can simply say the athlete passed. You will need to make some of these decisions as you progress and prepare. If you feel a crucial piece of information is missing, please contact your instructor in advance.

Finally, you have worked with this coach over the previous year. This is the start of your second year.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the coach needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

There are different reasons that a coach versus an AT as the EP for this simulation may be preferred. A coach may be more real in these interactions but may ask less direct health care–related questions and may not know when something is incorrect. An AT may not take on the role of a coach well and refuse to participate in the athlete’s care or emulate the other stresses or concerns of a coach. Additionally, an AT may know more than a coach, and this could change the simulation. I have found both to be effective, and it mostly depends on the person. In the past, I have chosen the EP based on my learning goals for that group students.

For this simulation, I always have the students prepare for all three situations and do not tell them their situation until about 24 hours prior to the simulation.
