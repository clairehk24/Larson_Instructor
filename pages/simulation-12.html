Simulation 12

Modalities Shuffle: Electrotherapy and Ultrasound

Instructor Simulation Summary

Health care providers (HCPs) will be assigned a modality case in advance of the simulation. This advance preparation will allow one student to participate as a patient, a second to serve as a proctor, and the third to participate as the HCP. Each HCP will complete three stations with a brief case at each station, requiring the HCP to decide on and use a modality to treat the injury. The HCP will answer the patient’s and the proctor’s questions before moving to the next scheduled station.

Instructor-Only Information

This simulation is designed to have one student play the role of a patient, a second play the role of the proctor, and a third to play the role of a health care provider (HCP), with one facilitator in the room. Each station should have a video camera to record the encounters so that the facilitator can review each individual encounter after the simulation.

You will distribute the same standardized patient (SP) and proctor information to each pair of students playing the patient and the proctor, but be sure you distribute only one patient case to each pair. Student HCPs should not know in advance any other pair’s case.

Scenario Synopsis

This simulation is designed to prepare an HCP to provide care to their patients when they have access to electrical modality units. Six stations will be set up for this simulation. HCPs will be divided into groups of two and assigned one station, where they must learn the patient case and appropriate modality parameters as a patient/proctor. Students will portray the patient/proctor for each other. The modality options for this simulation are electrical stimulation (including a transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation [TENS] unit) and therapeutic ultrasound. Students will rotate through three of the stations that they are not assigned as patient/proctor. Each station will have a treatment table. Once a student has moved through their three assigned stations, they may exit the room.

Content Area

Students must have an understanding of health informatics, therapeutic modalities, and developing patient care plans.

Learning Objectives

  1. Formulate a treatment goal and communicate it to the patient
  2. Select appropriate modalities to treat the patients’ problems
  3. Select appropriate modality parameters that align with the treatment goal
  4. Correctly apply the modality, including checking for contraindications, educating, and preparing the patient
  5. Justify each modality selection through answering clarifying questions
  6. Create and document a treatment plan in the electronic patient care record (PCR)

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 64
  • Standard 69
  • Standard 73

HCP Prebrief

HCPs 1-6 will start with the roles of patient and proctor. Halfway through the simulation, HCPs 7-12 will become the patient and proctor. This will allow each person to participate as the HCP three times. As the HCP, you will have a brief scenario about the nature of the injury, and then you must determine which modality to perform, explain the treatment goal and the treatment to the patient, and prepare them for the treatment. You will be evaluated on your selection of the modality, your patient communication, and your parameter settings. Please justify your modality choice as the best modality to meet the patients’ needs, indications, and contraindications. You may select a different modality than the one indicated as the best choice, as long as you have appropriately justified and selected a safe and useful modality for the condition.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing method from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

This simulation involves multiple scenarios with brief opportunities for clinical decision making. As such, the Diamond debriefing method may be useful to articulate and address any clinical and application-based questions

You may also consider the following prompts to facilitate your debriefing session:

Describe how you arrived at your decisions for each modality choice.

How did the description of the injury help you determine a modality choice?

How confident did you feel in your parameter selection for each modality?

What areas of improvement do you see that need to be addressed in your own performance?

Why are professional communication and demeanor necessary even in a routine modality treatment?

Describe your feelings about performing modalities on and being rated by your peer. How did you feel about rating your peer’s performance?

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

This simulation could be modified to include any modality students learn in their therapeutic modality courses. If you are interested in a simulation asking students to select from minimal modality options, superficial hot or cold for sites with no electric modalities, see Simulation 11: Modalities Shuffle: Cold and Hot. Simulation 11 has a different variation of the schedule, so you may choose either schedule for either simulation. That simulation does not use electrical modalities.

When having students serve as patients, it is important to give extra attention to other details so that students don’t slip into familiarity and treat the situation as any other day in class. In previous experiences, when the simulation does not create unique or unfamiliar circumstances, the patients complain about HCPs not taking the case seriously or HCPs talking directly to the facilitator, not the patients. When using students as patients, try to do this simulation in a new space (as opposed to the classroom), ensure the patients are dressed in a manner that resembles their case, consider them having different names, sports, institutions, and so on.

This simulation can be performed with actual SPs and proctors, as opposed to students, for each case. If this format is chosen, one SP and one proctor per case would still be the preferred method of conducting the simulation. This would also allow more cases per allotted time frame, enabling earlier completion or allowing students to encounter more cases.

If you have more than 12 students and wish to add an additional station, you could choose to include the following information in a seventh station.

Optional Station 7 Information

Read to HCP: “I am a cross-country athlete coming in after practice, complaining about my right hamstring contracting nonstop, and it is very painful. About seven months ago, I had a grade 2 hamstring strain or injury during the off-season. The pain was an 8/10 when I strained it. I’ve never had muscle spasms before, but they keep happening, mostly when I activate my hamstrings against resistance. It often happens when rolling over in bed, pulling my heel toward my body, or when tired. I have an abnormal gait and am unable to completely straighten my right leg. I’m tender to palpation along my right hamstring and gastrocnemius. What available modalities or therapies will be helpful to stop or reduce the contraction or spasm?”

Following are the parameters the HCP should select for this case, with the primary treatment goal of relieving pain and reducing or stopping the contraction or spasm.

Premodulated Interferential Electrical Stimulation Parameters

Treatment time: 10 to 30 minutes

Waveform: Premodulation

Carrier frequency: > 1,000 KHz

Pulse width: 150 to 200 µs

Pulse rate: 2 to 4 pps (low) >50 pps (high)

Intensity: Moderate visible muscle contraction

Electrode placement: Two electrodes on the motor point and muscle belly of the target muscle

Patient position: Patient prone

Motor TENS Parameters

Treatment time: 10 to 30 minutes

Mode: Burst or modulation

Pulse width: 150 to 200 µs

Pulse rate: 2 to 10 pps

Intensity: Moderate visible muscle contraction

Electrode placement: Two electrodes on the motor point and muscle belly of the target muscle

Patient position: Patient prone

What Are the Physiological Effects?

Involuntary muscle contraction from the premodulation or TENS fatigues the muscle, decreasing neuron signals activating the hamstring muscles and thus allowing them to relax.
