Simulation 30

Breaking Bad News

Instructor Simulation Summary

Health care providers (HCPs) will complete a patient consult where they will discuss the diagnosis findings with their patient.

Instructor-Only Information

This simulation involves a one-on-one private but recorded interaction with the health care provider (HCP) and a patient. The students will review the interaction with the patient and then score themselves on how well they performed during the interaction based on the SPIKES protocol1 and other important factors. The rubric they will complete could simply be turned in to the instructor of the course, or preferably, a one-on-one meeting can be scheduled, and the student can illustrate how the rubric matches their performance. If you choose a different protocol, you can adapt this rubric to that protocol.

Scenario Synopsis

This simulation is set in a provider’s office. The athletic trainer (AT) has accompanied the athlete for results on an imaging test. The athlete has asked the AT to accompany them to the office and discuss the test results. After the AT meets with the physician, the AT will present the diagnosis of grade 3 cervical degenerative disc disease (DDD) at C5/C6 and C6/C7 levels or cervical stenosis around C2 to C5 of the spine to their patient. According to the six-step SPIKES (Setting up, Perception, Invitation, Knowledge, Emotions and Empathy, Strategy and Summary) protocol for delivering bad news, the best person to discuss this information with the patient is the provider most familiar with the patient, which, in this case, is you, the HCP. Be prepared to discuss this information with the patient in the manner suggested by the SPIKES protocol or a similar protocol.

Content Area

Understanding of cervical neck injuries and patient-centered communication skills.

Learning Objectives

  1. Effectively communicate the diagnosis to a patient using clear language in layperson’s terms while demonstrating empathy
  2. Exhibit empathy and professionalism through addressing patients’ emotional and psychological concerns while fostering a supportive environment
  3. Engage the patient with the decision-making process by discussing various treatment options and considering the patient’s preferences in the development of the plan of care
  4. Engage in postdiagnosis lifestyle discussions and options for an active, healthy lifestyle
  5. Demonstrate active listening to effectively address the patient’s questions and concerns
  6. Accurately document the patient consultation, including the diagnosis, discussion of treatment options, and patient preferences for the plan of care

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 59
  • Standard 64

HCP Prebrief

This simulation will involve a patient encounter in an office setting where you will serve as an HCP. You will greet your patient in the lobby of the practice and then take them to another area to discuss the imaging results. After about 10 minutes, a physician will enter the room to answer any unanswered questions. This physician role may be played by your instructor or someone else. The interaction will be recorded. The entire encounter will last up to 15 minutes. Immediately prior to the interaction, you will be told which diagnosis exists for your patient: cervical stenosis or DDD.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

Given the amount of self-reflection opportunities in this simulation, students may benefit from utilizing the Reflective Practice in Health Care debriefing model.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

You may consider having a proctor in the room, but it is likely the HCP will deflect questions, emotions, and situation difficulties to the other person in the room unless the role of this person is chosen wisely or the student is informed that the person is not available to assist.
