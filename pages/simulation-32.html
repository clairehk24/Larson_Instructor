Simulation 32

It All Comes Together at the Hip

Instructor Simulation Summary

The purpose of this simulation is for health care providers (HCPs) to demonstrate the ability to evaluate and treat a chronic hip injury.

Instructor-Only Information

This simulation is designed to allow students to put information together from multiple courses across their curriculum. Instructors may choose to ask the students to complete the evaluation in this simulation and then perform a therapeutic intervention (either therapeutic exercise, modality, or both) as time and instructional goals allow.

Scenario Synopsis

This simulation takes place in a clinical setting. The patient is a 40-year-old recreational marathon runner and triathlete. The patient presents to the clinic with low back and right-sided upper hamstring pain that has persisted on and off for the past year. Despite self-taught home exercises and strengthening, the patient’s condition has not improved, and so, they are seeking professional evaluation and treatment.

Content Area

Students must have musculoskeletal evaluation and therapeutic intervention knowledge.

Learning Objectives

  1. Conduct a thorough musculoskeletal evaluation
  2. Communicate with a patient to explain their diagnosis
  3. Develop a plan of care for a home exercise program for a patient
  4. Select appropriate modality, therapeutic intervention, or both, to treat the patient’s injury
  5. Select appropriate modality, therapeutic exercise parameters, or both that align with the plan of care

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 59
  • Standard 69
  • Standard 71
  • Standard 73
  • Standard 82

Simulation Situation

This simulation will involve a one-on-one patient evaluation and initial treatment selection.

Setting

Therapy clinic

HCP Prebrief

This simulation will take up to 18 minutes. You are tasked with assessing and treating a patient with hip pain and beginning the initial treatment plan.

When you have successfully addressed the injury and treated the patient, you can leave.

If you do not complete within 18 minutes, you will be told, “Time is up.” You must stop where you are and leave the simulation area. The proctor may ask, “Is there anything you would like to add?” and may ask clarifying questions.

You may be asked to clarify a differential diagnosis or other criteria. This should not be interpreted as doing good or bad. These are questions that may be asked of everyone for clarification or other purposes.

Please return your equipment to the starting area where you received it. Also, please be sure all equipment is returned to its original state or replaced, if needed. If something is broken, please inform the simulation assistant when you return it or go into the supply room or cabinet and replace.

Treat this person as a real patient. You should provide care as you would in an actual situation. If you are unclear, you can always ask the proctor.

If there is a piece of equipment that you need and it is not available, you can verbalize that piece of equipment and its use.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Other comments:

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

This simulation does not necessarily lend itself to a particular debriefing method, but you may find the Diamond debriefing method to be useful in your debrief.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

Due to the length of each simulation encounter, instructors may wish to hire multiple SPs to portray this patient simultaneously in separate rooms. This simulation has a sample schedule of two separate rooms and one SP in each, portraying the same case. Depending on the size of your class, you may wish to utilize one or multiple rooms to facilitate this simulation. For the safety of the SP and the student, we do recommend having a proctor in each simulation room.
