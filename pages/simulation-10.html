Simulation 10

History Assessment Simulation Series (HASS)

Instructor Simulation Summary

This simulation involves numerous cases that utilize the health care provider’s (HCP’s) history-taking skills. Each situation is time-limited to one minute for entry information, four minutes for patient assessment, and two minutes to relay the history information back to a head athletic trainer (AT) or proctor. A thorough history may take longer, but this activity requires students to ask targeted history questions.

Instructor-Only Information

This simulation will have multiple, up to five, situations available for use. The goal of each situation is to quickly gather a patient history and then convey that information back to another provider, head athletic trainer (AT) or proctor. See Simulation Variations and Author Notes for two variations that do not require standardized patients (SPs).

Scenario Synopsis

The goal of each situation in this simulation is to determine the patient’s medical need and history enough to make decisions about differential diagnoses, referrals, emergencies, or treatments. It has been said that obtaining a good medical history is 80 percent or more of diagnosing a condition.

There are multiple stations. During this simulation, the following situation will occur at each station. You will be asked something similar by the proctor portraying a head or lead HCP, “Please see what that patient needs. Do not touch the patient; just figure out what is going on and what is needed. I will check back with you in four minutes.” After your patient assessment, you will then inform the lead HCP of what you discovered.

Remember, it is not always important to diagnose. You will always have situations you don’t understand or for which you have not been educated. It is most important to understand which body system is affected, then ask as many related pertinent and relevant questions or inspect and observe (without touching) to understand how this condition is manifesting in the patient.

Content Area

HCPs should be able to take a comprehensive history and report on important details. The HCP does not need to know or understand some of the conditions but must be able to gather relevant information and convey this information to the lead HCP.

Learning Objectives

Gather a relevant patient history

Provide a differential diagnosis

Alert the lead HCP to any concerning conditions or presentations

Relay a patient’s history to a lead HCP

Complete a minimum history within four minutes

Assess without touching a patient

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

It can be more educational to put the students in small groups and require each group to make up the case and rubric. This has been an excellent activity. The cases need to be reviewed and vetted by the instructor before allowing the students to use them in the simulation to ensure the cases include a history, are educational, and are informative. On the day of the simulation, a few groups will go first, and all students will visit those groups. Then, it will transition, and the other groups will lead, and all the students will visit them. Students really enjoy seeing the ideas, processes, and skills of their peers and find it very educational to create and to watch and grade their colleagues. Creating the case requires them to think through what they expect to happen, how it will happen, and to prepare and practice this prior to the simulation day. I will usually suggest they use a situation they are familiar with or have experienced or to find a case study.

If personnel is an issue, there are many variations. You can use the variation discussed earlier. You can simply choose to do one or two situations. You can have one SP be the patient for all students, and then switch to a new case. In this way, one SP can do two cases. You could use some students as patients for a couple of cases. Then, at some point, switch and use a new students for the other cases.
