Simulation 22

Provider Communication (Head Injury Part 3)

Instructor Simulation Summary

This simulation involves a phone call with a physical therapist (PT) or other health care provider (HCP) to whom you referred a patient with persistent concussion symptoms.

Instructor-Only Information

The decision to refer to a physical therapist (PT) for persistent concussion symptoms may be unique to a state, institution, available resources, or for a variety of other reasons. While a referral to a neurologist may be appropriate, a referral to a PT, particularly a PT specializing in vestibular, neurologic, or concussion symptoms, is appropriate and well within a PT’s training, education, and skill set. Additionally, some states allow for direct referral to a PT, which may be quicker, less expensive, and more in line with what is needed for the athlete. The PT is well-trained to know when a neurologist referral is necessary. If this is uncommon, unfamiliar, or will not work for you, consider referral to a different health care provider (HCP).

Part of this simulation is to educate not only athletic trainers (ATs) on the skills and scope of PTs but also for the PTs participating in the simulation to get a better understanding of the skills and scope of ATs and to cultivate a robust working relationship. In this way, the simulation is a form of promotion or advocacy for ATs. This simulation is the third part in a series of simulations for a concussion, Head-to-Head (Part 1, Simulation 20), and Sport Concussion Assessment Tool (Part 2, Simulation 21). It is not necessary to participate in these prior to this simulation, but doing all three provides a great learning opportunity for the student to experience the continuum of care. In this simulation, the PT believes that care can continue with the AT as long as the AT feels confident in providing care and the AT can appropriately answer the questions of the PT. The goal is for the AT to explain to the PT why the AT is a preferred provider for an athlete with an uncomplicated concussion or a more complex concussion after a referral and as part of a team, as is the case here. The AT should explain the return-to-play and return-to-learn process and should provide information on when or if the PT should be contacted or involved in care in the future. Finally, the student AT will collaborate with the PT on specific tools, strategies, or processes for diminishing symptoms and improving outcomes for the patient.

This simulation also involves documentation as part of student presimulation activities. If you plan to have students document this case using a patient care report (PCR), but you do not yet have one, you can create your documentation requirements via paper or other electronic resources.

Scenario Synopsis

The purpose of this simulation is for the HCP to demonstrate the ability to make a referral to and a plan with a PT for a patient with atypical length concussion symptoms (unchanged after 96 hours). This patient (Rain Shoemaker) experienced a head-to-head collision during soccer practice, was evaluated for a concussion the following day, but the symptoms have not resolved or diminished 96 hours postconcussion. This phone call occurs after the PT’s evaluation and involves the decision for care to return to the athletic trainer (AT), stay with the PT, or some combination of the two. A specific plan for the patient should be created or outlined during this interaction.

Content Area

Knowledge of concussion injury; management, assessment, treatment, and rehabilitation options for a concussion, including return-to-play (RTP) and return-to-learn (RTL) criteria. Knowledge of the role of this HCP and the PT for concussion and ability to discuss strengths and weaknesses of the HCP in the athlete’s rehabilitation and recovery. Able to collaborate and plan with other HCPs to decrease symptoms and improve outcomes.

Learning Objectives

  1. Create a patient referral (document, email, phone call)
  2. Discuss the HCP’s unique ability and skill set for managing a concussion of an athlete
  3. Outline critical points in the concussed athlete’s care that require referral or outside intervention
  4. Coordinate with a PT to determine the best course of action for a specific athlete
  5. Educate the other HCP on the RTP and RTL criteria for this athlete
  6. Gather information from the other HCP, including specific skills or tools, that can be used to reduce symptoms and improve outcomes
  7. Document to the provider via email (and in an electronic medical record [EMR]) the results of the assessment, plan for this athlete, and any red flags or referral criteria agreed upon during the interaction
  8. Demonstrate knowledge and ability to follow protected health information (PHI) rules when interacting with another HCP

Standardized Patient Information

There is no standardized patient (SP) for this simulation. The patient being referred to had a head-to-head collision (Simulation 20: Head-to-Head), was evaluated for a concussion the next morning (Simulation 21: Sport Concussion Assessment Tool), and is now being referred to a PT and being discussed over the phone, but no one is actually evaluating the patient. The instructions for the PT are in the Embedded Personnel document.

HCP Prebrief

The phone call will last no longer than 10 minutes. (The phone call may be recorded.)

The PT will be calling you from the phone number (xxx-xxx-xxxx).

Once the simulation has ended or time is up, the PT will hang up. There is no feedback at this time.

There will be a debrief in the future.

Be sure to ask your instructor or look for postsimulation assignments and requirements. You will likely be asked to contact the PT with a summary of the call and the action plan for the patient.

SAFETY: The physical therapist will say “Time-out; time-out” if they need to stop the simulation for any reason. If this happens, stop the simulation, ask if anything is needed, and if not, hang up and contact your instructor for further instructions.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

There are many debriefing models that are appropriate for this simulation, but since students will be reflecting on a phone call with another HCP, the +Delta method may be an appropriate debriefing model. However, please refer to the debriefing table on HKPropel to select the most appropriate debriefing model for your experience and goals.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

For this simulation, the AT could meet in person with a PT. Additionally, the actual patient could be present and either be evaluated or be part of the discussion.

If you wish to substitute a different HCP instead of referring to a PT, you might use a mental health therapist, neurologist, or a sports medicine–trained physician assistant or nurse practitioner.
