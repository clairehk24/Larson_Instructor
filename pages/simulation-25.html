Simulation 25

Foot Pain Telehealth

Instructor Simulation Summary

During this simulation, a high school athlete calls their health care provider (HCP) from home to discuss foot pain after a soccer match.

Instructor-Only Information

This simulation involves an undiagnosed stress fracture for a minor athlete who is home alone on a Friday night. The athlete has no viable transportation option to see the health care provider (HCP) in the near future, and it would involve a lot of walking, causing pain. The athlete has plans to go on a family vacation tomorrow with lots of activities scheduled. When the mother arrives home that evening, if the HCP states imaging is necessary, it would involve a trip to the emergency room (ER) at midnight. Some of the difficulty in this simulation is the minor’s access to care and determining if care is necessary immediately or how inconvenient it is to obtain. This simulation is written with the proctor as the patient.

Scenario Synopsis

A high school sophomore soccer athlete, who is expected to be a significant scorer, calls the HCP two hours postgame on a Friday afternoon. It is about 6 p.m. The athlete explains that about 15 minutes after the game, as the endorphins from winning started to wear off, the athlete noticed their foot was hurting. The athlete did not want to stop celebrating and so blew it off, assuming it would get better. After a shower, the athlete went home to rest but later called the team’s athletic trainer (AT), the HCP, because the pain had not subsided.

Content Area

Ability to read and understand literature on different foot conditions. Anatomical knowledge of the foot and bony landmarks. Ability to provide guidelines or recommendations for activity restrictions with a foot injury. Use clinical reasoning to convey an appropriate level of urgency to the athlete. Communicate appropriately and effectively with a minor and their family.

Learning Objectives

  1. Participate in a phone conversation (telehealth) regarding a musculoskeletal condition
  2. Identify an emergent or nonemergent situation
  3. Recognize and balance the patient’s needs and desires while providing options that clarify the consequences of the choices made by the patient
  4. Demonstrate interest and caring for the patient by listening and using empathy and understanding
  5. Identify and discuss next steps for care and provide appropriate management and referral options (both current and future)
  6. Use email communication to a parent to summarize the evaluation, assessment, and plan of care for an injured minor

HCP Prebrief

The phone call will last approximately 12 to 15 minutes. (The phone call may be recorded.)

Your patient will be calling you from the phone number (xxx-xxx-xxxx). You will answer the call and treat them as a real patient.

You will have up to 15 minutes to complete this simulation. You do not need to use all your time.

Once the simulation has ended or time is up, the SP will hang up.

There will be a debrief in the future.

Be sure to ask your instructor or look for postsimulation assignments and requirements.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

Since this simulation may not involve the facilitator directly observing the encounters, it may be beneficial to utilize the +Delta or a three-phase model such as the Gather, analyze, summarize (GAS).

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

One of the preparation activities includes some questions regarding cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR). The authors sometimes find it useful and educational to put other topics into the activities to keep the students on their toes, so to speak, and to be sure they are refreshed on many topics. If you are planning to implement Simulation 3, Emergency Action Plan (EAP) after this simulation, assigning this presimulation activity may help your students prepare for EAP simulation without giving away the surprise (see Simulation 3, Emergency Action Plan (EAP) for more information on the cardiac arrest simulation).

HIPAA or FERPA information is not included or addressed in this simulation. These could easily be added, and content to address this could be useful. It would provide a useful variation.
