Simulation 1

Time-Out Ankle

Instructor Simulation Summary

This simulation is used to assess health care providers’ (HCPs) ability to conduct a quick ankle evaluation, make a decision for return to play (RTP), and apply taping for a grade I or II ankle sprain.

Instructor-Only Information

There will be two athlete situations. One is likely a return-to-play (RTP) athlete with moderate symptoms and may require ankle taping. The second player’s injury is likely to appear too severe, and the health care provider (HCP) may determine that the player needs to forfeit the match. The time frame for the assessment and the sport can be chosen by the instructor. This simulation references wrestling and the current medical time-out rule for wrestling. Whichever sport, rules, and timings are chosen, this information must be reviewed and confirmed with the HCPs.

The medical time-out rules suggested for this simulation and introduced to students in presimulation Activity 3 were chosen partially from the Wisconsin Interscholastic Athletic Association (WIAA) Wrestling Information for Medical Personnel. These rules state that an injured or ill athlete may have two time-outs that are a cumulative 90 seconds throughout the match. For this simulation, you can call a second 90-second time-out or extend the time-out to 4 minutes to apply prophylactic taping. The WIAA states that taping that “substantially restricts the normal movement of a joint shall be prohibited.”

Additionally, see the Simulation Variations and Author Notes for a similar situation applied to tennis, which also has specific time allowances for injuries.

Scenario Synopsis

An athlete rolls their ankle during a wrestling competition. The HCP takes the first 90-second injury time-out to evaluate the ankle and determine if the athlete should RTP or if they should forfeit the match. If deemed necessary, the HCP may request an extension to the injury time-out by an additional 90 seconds or ask for a 4-minute time-out to select and apply tape to return the athlete to the match.

Content Area

Students must be familiar with orthopedic evaluation, including selecting and using special tests. Students must also be able to apply ankle taping and communicate with the athlete and personnel regarding RTP decisions.

Learning Objectives

  1. Perform an evaluation for an ankle sprain within 90 seconds
  2. Make an RTP decision with the athlete
  3. Return the athlete to competition within the required time frame
  4. Select and apply a lateral ankle tape procedure (if indicated) by the end of five minutes

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 59
  • Standard 71
  • Standard 78

HCP Prebrief

Today’s simulation will resemble a live wrestling match injury time-out where you have limited time to evaluate and make an RTP decision with your patient. Here are the details for the presenting scenario.

It is the third period of the match, and the score is 10 to 7 with your patient in the lead. This is the high school wrestling semifinal round. If your patient wins, they will proceed to the final to compete as the top wrestler in the country.

When you make your RTP decision, be specific about why you have chosen to hold the patient out or why they can safely return.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

For this simulation, we recommend selecting a three-phase debriefing method such as the reaction, analysis, and summary (RAS)1; the gather, analyze, and summarize (GAS)2; or the Diamond technique, which includes the phases of description, analysis, and application.3

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

You may choose to use this simulation as a tennis match that has specific medical time-out rules with a similar injury time-out requirement.

This simulation could be used to assess students’ readiness for a clinical site or used as a practice opportunity for speed and efficiency with evaluations and taping under pressure.

If needed, this simulation could be extended to allow students more time and then gradually decrease the time frame as they progress in their skills.

This simulation required students to prepare their medical kits with first aid supplies, like a traditional wrestling match preparation. To simplify the simulation, you may ask the students to bring only taping supplies or have them prepared in advance.
