Simulation 16

A Good Mimic

Instructor Simulation Summary

The health care provider (HCP) will evaluate a patient complaining of ankle pain and swelling.

Instructor-Only Information

This is a tabletop-style simulation that uses slides. It is coordinated like a solve-the-mystery simulation. There is no SP or other personnel required during this simulation other than the instructor. During the simulation, a slide will be shown to the students to prompt a discussion or participation in activities. Initially, the health care provider (HCP) will evaluate ankle pain and swelling in an athlete. As the situation progresses, medical conditions are evaluated. The patient assumes the medical condition is gout based on family history and presentation. During two of the slides, instructors will provide a note card with patient information to students (one of a pair of students who is portraying the patient), and the other will play the role of HCP. Later, students will switch roles, and a new note card with new patient information will be provided to the patient-student, while the other plays the role of HCP. After further investigation, the athlete is found to have cellulitis. The HCPs are unaware of how this case will develop, so you will play a role in guiding their choices on what to investigate. Different medications and interactions are evaluated during the diagnosis. During the assessment, students explore stereotypes, race, and health care bias.

It is expected that all nonharmful views will be heard, expressed, and discussed by the students and the instructor. In our experience, the students are willing to express and discuss divergent, confusing, or controversial issues without any problems and with respect. It may be important to remind students of the National Athletic Trainers’ Association (NATA) code of conduct, ethics, and the role of HCPs to provide care to their patients. PPT slides have been provided for you to use in leading the simulation.

Scenario Synopsis

While on the road with the team, an athlete experiences lateral and posterior ankle pain. The athlete is taking other medications for other conditions and experiences flu-like symptoms during the simulation.

Content Area

Students should have ankle examination and assessment skills. Content is related to general medicine and pharmacology. Some familiarity with medication actions, dispensing, and general medical conditions will be helpful. Students should be willing and able to discuss topics of diversity and health care, as well as the National Collegiate Athletic Association’s (NCAA’s) role in athletes’ ability to compete and participate in higher education.

Learning Objectives

  1. Discuss expressions of culture, ethnicity, and race, particularly in athletics and health care
  2. Discuss the role or relationship of the NCAA in athletics and health care
  3. Understand the dose, indication, contraindication, route, and adverse events of medications that may be used by an athlete
  4. Select and dispense medications appropriate for athlete’s presentation and condition
  5. Gather a history, assess a patient, and perform interventions relevant to the presenting conditions

Debrief

In general, this simulation is discussed as it occurs, and a separate debrief is not required. However, if topics during the simulation seem unresolved or unsettling, you may want to allow 15 minutes for students to simply provide final thoughts and clinical takeaways as a way to end the simulation.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

This simulation could easily be adapted to a different sport, venue, or other political actions. This simulation involves topics that may be difficult for students to discuss openly and safely. These conversations are important but may be challenging and require respect and civility. A simple list from the Teaching Engagement Program lists the following things to consider.
