Simulation 21

Sport Concussion Assessment Tool (Head Injury Part 2)

Instructor Simulation Summary

Health care providers (HCPs) will evaluate a patient with a suspected concussion. See HKPropel for the patient’s Sport Concussion Assessment Tool (SCAT6).

Instructor-Only Information

If you previously used the head-to-head simulation, Simulation 20, this simulation utilizes the same patients from that situation. If you plan to have students document this case using a patient care report (PCR), but you do not yet have one, you can create your documentation requirements via paper or other electronic resources. If students previously documented this patient in Simulation 20, they can return to the patient PCR to continue documentation (see Learning Objective 5). If you did not do Part 1 (Simulation 20), you can simply choose the standardized patient (SP) case you would like to use for the students (or do both). If you did Part 1, see the Simulation Variations and Author Notes in Simulation 20 or Simulation 21 on how to keep the same patient with the same health care provider (HCP).

Note that the HCP Patient Information is available to you but not to your students, so that if they are doing Simulation 20, they cannot look ahead to what the results of Simulation 20 should be. You should release the HCP Patient Information to students after they complete Simulation 20, or whenever, if they will not complete Simulation 20 first. The Sport Concussion Assessment Tool (SCAT6) for HCPs’ use is available on HKPropel, with the file name HCP SCAT6 Rain Shoemaker.pdf.

Scenario Synopsis

The student will use the SCAT6 to assess for a concussion on an athlete who sustained an injury from a head-to-head contact the previous morning. The information from that incident can be found in Simulation 20. It is also referenced in portions of “HCP SCAT6 Rain Shoemaker.pdf” on HKPropel.

Content Area

Students should have knowledge of how to manage and assess a concussion, including return-to-play (RTP) criteria.

Learning Objectives

  1. Administer a SCAT6 (the day after injury)
  2. Determine whether other tests are appropriate or needed and if or when to stop testing
  3. Arrive at a differential diagnosis for the patient
  4. Outline a return-to-play (RTP) and return-to-learn (RTL) plan for this patient
  5. Document case in the patient care report (PCR), including RTP and RTL, SCAT6, and all other relevant assessments or notes for this encounter

HCP Prebrief

Please be sure you have read about the prior incident and understand the patient’s previous history. Your instructor will distribute the HCP initial injury patient information, or you can always ask the patient for clarification.

You will have 20 minutes to assess the patient for a concussion.

The examination tools you need are present in this exam room. If there is something you cannot find, please speak to a facilitator.

Treat this person as a real patient.

You will be required to document your evaluation today and your findings. As part of that, you can record your SCAT6 in any manner you wish, but a completed SCAT6 will be required in your patient documentation.

At the completion of your assessment, please complete the PCR for this patient or speak to your instructor on how to document your care today.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

There are many debriefing models appropriate for this simulation, but instructors may find one of the three phase debriefing models useful.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

If the head-to-head simulation was conducted previously but not continuously with this simulation, the HCP can see the same patient (not necessarily the same SP) they saw in the head-to-head simulation, Simulation 20 (Head Injury Part 1). Be sure to discuss and practice this when training the SPs. On the simulation day, be sure your schedule indicates which patient the HCP saw during the head-to-head simulation. During the rehearsal and the day of the simulation, simply give the SP a uniquely colored note card that lists the patient’s name, nickname, and symptoms (B = Nosebleed, tongue bleed, temple pain, went home or Skittle = Vomit, hospital, negative scan or head injury, went home). Then, when the HCP asks questions, they will have prompts and notes to use and will know which patient they are with and which HCP is present. If B and Skittle have different color note cards, then without reading the note card, the SP and the proctor will know which patient they are portraying for that situation.
