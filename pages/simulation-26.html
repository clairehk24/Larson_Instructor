Simulation 26

Foot Pain Gait Analysis

Instructor Simulation Summary

Health care providers (HCPs) will complete an observational gait analysis and foot posture assessment and fit the patient for an orthotic.

Instructor-Only Information

This simulation involves students viewing a prerecorded video of their patient’s running gait before the simulation begins, by completing either presimulation Activity 4, 5, or 6. If you choose to have your students complete presimulation Activity 5, you will need to schedule the standardized patient (SP) time to meet with your students to have their gait recorded in advance of the simulation.

Scenario Synopsis

This simulation is a follow-up from a previous simulation (Simulation 25), where a patient called the HCP with a foot injury, that eventually was diagnosed as a navicular stress fracture. In this simulation, students will view a prerecorded video of the patients’ running gait before the simulation begins. Students will then enter the room as the clinician, ready to discuss the running gait analysis with the standardized patient (SP) who is sitting on the exam table. It is not necessary to participate in Simulation 25: Foot Pain Telehealth, but this is theoretically the same patient.

Content Area

Students must be familiar with gait and foot posture analysis as well as orthotic fitting, fabrication, or both.

Learning Objectives

  1. Conduct an observational gait analysis of a patient’s running gait video
  2. Perform a foot posture analysis
  3. Discuss analysis findings and intervention recommendations with the patient
  4. Perform orthotic fitting or fabrication

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 59
  • Standard 71
  • Standard 73
  • Standard 78

HCP Prebrief

This simulation will involve a patient consult, assessment, and orthotic fitting. When you have finished, you may leave the room.

If you do not complete within 15 minutes, you will be told, “Time is up.” You must stop where you are and leave the simulation area.

Please return your equipment to the starting area where you received it. Also, please be sure all equipment is returned to its original state or replaced, if needed. If something is broken, please inform the simulation assistant when you return it or go into the supply room or cabinet and replace.

Treat this person as a real patient. You should provide care as you would in an actual situation. If you are unclear, you can always ask the proctor.

As a reminder, the person or foot you see in any video or photo will not be the same person or foot you see in the patient today. You are treating the foot, the problems, and the gait seen in the video. If you have questions about this or if something is unclear, please ask the patient or proctor for clarity. The fiction contract should be followed, and the patient should be treated as the person or foot in the video or photo.

There is a two-minute transition between HCPs. Please leave the room quickly, replace all supplies to their location and return the space to its original condition.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

Similar to Simulation 25, we recommend utilizing either the +Delta or a three-phase debriefing model to facilitate the debriefing session for this simulation.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

This simulation may be held as part 2 of Foot Pain Telehealth, Simulation 25, so students are familiar with the patient and their background going into this simulation. Alternatively, this simulation may stand alone because it has the needed background information to allow the students to succeed in the gait, posture analysis, and orthotic fitting or fabrication. Facilitators may choose to have students simply fit a foam mold impression for an orthotic for the patient, or they may schedule an additional 10 minutes per student to have each student fabricate a thermoplastic orthotic for their patient.

This simulation involves students analyzing a video by completing either presimulation Activity 4, 5, or 6. As the instructor, you may choose the activity the students will complete and, therefore, the video recording they will view prior to their SP encounter.
