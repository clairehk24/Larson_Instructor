Simulation 18

Cervical Spine Injury

Instructor Simulation Summary

This situation will occur in a county ice arena. The health care providers (HCP) are visiting, per diem athletic trainers (AT) for an amateur ice-skating competition. The competition is for ages 16 and up. Today is practice, and the competition is tomorrow and the following day. During a practice, one athlete was dropped on their head.

Instructor-Only Information

This simulation includes the option to collaborate with emergency medical services (EMS) and to transfer care with EMS. In this simulation, it is done cooperatively. However, that is not always the case, and the EMS embedded personnel (EP) can challenge the choices of health care providers (HCPs). This case also has an option for a parent to be an EP. In this simulation, the parent is concerned and worried but not confrontational or difficult. Both these options can be added individually, together, or not at all. If you choose to add one of these EPs, you could either choose to make it a surprise that a parent and EMS will arrive, or you can choose to inform the students that they will have a conversation with a parent, EMS, or both. This simulation is also written for the proctor to be the event manager as an EP, but it can be a separate person. Finally, it is rarely a surprise who is in charge or what role each HCP has in a situation such as this. For this reason, the HCPs are instructed to choose roles, but you could assign specific roles. Instructions and suggestions to students on roles are provided in the HCP Prebrief. Unless this is a surprise or formative assessment, students should be given this information well in advance of the simulation.

Scenario Synopsis

HCPs will be assessing and providing care to an athlete who may have experienced a cervical spine injury (CSI). Students will be working in a team of four to six people.

Content Area

Students must be familiar with CSI and assessment, spinal motion restriction (SMR), working with a team, communicating with EMS, and communicating with a parent.

Learning Objectives

  1. Effectively communicate with the patient, parent, and health care team
  2. Work as a team to provide optimal patient care
  3. Advocate for the patient
  4. Consider HIPAA regulations and protect patient privacy
  5. Manage the patient using evidence-based practices like the NEXUS or Canadian C-spine rules

  • Standard 61
  • Standard 62

HCP Prebrief

This simulation will take up to 20 minutes. You are tasked with assessing and treating all conditions found.

You will be assigned to a group of HCPs. Please choose the role of each person in the group. Generally, there will be a lead provider, an assistant provider, and other providers. Additionally, the responsibilities of each provider varies according to the EAP or practice, but generally, it is as follows: one person will use spinal motion restriction for the cervical spine; one person is in charge of speaking to the patient and conveying patient information (these two roles may be the same); another person is responsible for managing other injuries or conditions and the physical exam; while another person gathers, distributes, or facilitates use of the equipment for SMR, and finally, another person records information (depending on the number of people in each group) and speaks to emergency medical services.

You may transfer care to another health care clinician, and you may need to speak to a parent.

If you are unclear about a patient presentation or what you feel, see, or hear, the proctor can answer your questions (the proctor and event manager may be the same person in this simulation).

If there is a piece of equipment that you need and it is not available, you can verbalize that piece of equipment and its use, or it will be provided.

You should provide care as you would in an actual situation but may need to verbalize some things for safety reasons.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that others need assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

Male pairs figure skating or ice dancing is not allowed in the International Skating Union. However, having a male skater be the person who is dropped is not a problem. It can be spun as a political statement, an event that allows role swapping, or does not prohibit same gender skating, or a woman that drops a man, and so on. This is up to you. It is also not uncommon in a simulation to explain to students that, unless sex or gender matters in this situation, you may have a male or female patient, and this may not match the description or sport. Please adhere to the fiction contract and treat this patient as you would any patient in this situation.

It is an interesting and challenging option to have the parent call the event manager and speak to one of the ATs by phone. This requires the AT to explain the situation clearly and to consider additional information not generally considered during an in-person interaction.

If you are unable or choose not to use a parent or EMS in this simulation, you can ask questions of the students related to these roles. “What is your transfer of care to EMS?” “Why did you choose to immobilize my child?” “What would you do if EMS decided it was not necessary?” “I got here as fast as I could. Where is my child?” “Do you have enough information from EMS to know where they are going and how the parent can locate their child?” “Would one of you choose to ride with EMS? Did you ask if that was OK?” Numerous questions could be asked of each provider.
