Simulation 14

Taping Ethics

Instructor Simulation Summary

Health care provider (HCP) will meet with two or more patients who need taping applications and may have ethical issues or questions.

Instructor-Only Information

This simulation involves taping skills, but each scenario also has an ethical component woven in for the students to manage. Trigger warning: Some of the scenarios contain information about verbal abuse, body shaming, and physical abuse.

Scenario Synopsis

The HCPs will be set up at taping table stations with a patient whom they must tape and communicate with. The setup for this simulation could mimic a typical athletic training room during prepractice or game taping where an athletic trainer has multiple patients to tape and speak to in a short period of time. During the interaction, the HCP will be presented with an ethical dilemma and must respond to the patient.

Content Area

Students should have intermediate- to advanced-level taping skills, advanced knowledge and understanding of managing ethical dilemmas, and proficient communication skills.

Learning Objectives

  1. Apply appropriate tape application for an injured body part
  2. Identify red flags in the patient’s behavior and conversation
  3. Provide care for an athlete who may not adhere to the same ethical principles or values to which an athletic trainer must adhere
  4. Discuss ethical dilemmas and professional values while providing patient-centered care

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 59
  • Standard 65
  • Standard 78

HCP Prebrief

Please review the simulation schedule. Please arrive at the preparation location at least 10 minutes prior to your first simulation.

You will be called into the simulation room when your taping station is ready. It could be prior to or after the scheduled time, depending on how things are moving. You will visit two to four stations. When you complete all stations, you are done. You can stay if you want to discuss anything with the instructors.

To start each station, the SP will say hello and start the timer. Then, they will give you a brief description of what they would like to have taped.

When the timer goes off or if you finish prior to the timer going off, return to the preparation area and either get a new patient or wait until it is time for you to go again. Please exit the simulation area when you are done or instructed that your time is over.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

This simulation is likely to elicit some initial reactions and more questions of students. Because of this, we recommend a longer debriefing session and instructors may find it useful to use the PEARLS debriefing tool as this simulation involved technical skills, decision making, communication, and resource utilization. If this simulation elicits emotions, brings up past trauma, or is stress-inducing for students, it may be useful to debrief using the Trauma-informed Psychologically Safe (TiPS) debriefing framework.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

This simulation could include any of the four provided SP cases, or you could further develop the ethical dilemmas provided in the student presimulation activities. Each scenario could be altered to be in a different setting (high school, college, professional, clinic) and could be altered to be with another team or different sport. We recommend students rotate between taping stations and complete at least two stations during the simulation.
