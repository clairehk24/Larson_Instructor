Simulation 28

A Day in the Life: Distractions in Rehabilitation

Instructor Simulation Summary

Health care providers (HCPs) will learn to effectively manage a rehabilitation session with various patients while managing patients’ concerns in conversation, including ethical dilemmas.

Instructor-Only Information

This simulation relies on students having completed presimulation Activities 4 and 6. Regardless of the other presimulation activities you assign, be sure to assign Activity 4: Therapeutic Intervention for Elbow and Activity 6: Policy Development so that students are prepared for these aspects of the simulation. Please also instruct students to bring their intervention plan and list of policies with them to the prebrief for the simulation. This simulation requires a facilitator. Typically, the instructor serves as the simulation facilitator, but you may also employ another faculty member to serve as facilitator.

Finally, this simulation requires coordination between standardized patients (SPs) and embedded personnel (EPs). It may be helpful for you to meet as a group with the SP/EP team so that each person understands who they will interact with during the simulation.

Scenario Synopsis

This simulation is set in a collegiate athletic training facility and is meant to resemble a rehabilitation session with multiple patients simultaneously. HCPs will work in pairs for this simulation. This will be a tabletop-style simulation where the simulation facilitators will pause and discuss events at different points during the simulation and provide opportunities for HCP interactions. This simulation will last 50 to 70 minutes, and all students will observe the simulation when not playing the role of head athletic trainer (AT) or other AT.

Content Area

Students should be able to perform therapeutic exercise interventions and educate patients on these interventions, as well as have the ability to tape various upper- and lower-extremity pathologies. Students should also have the knowledge of managing ethical dilemmas.

Learning Objectives

  1. Instruct a patient in the correct performance of prescribed therapeutic exercise interventions
  2. Modify therapeutic exercises as needed for success and harm reduction
  3. Promote a patient-provider rapport with a patient through open lines of communication
  4. Identify and address ethical concerns

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 59
  • Standard 65
  • Standard 73
  • Standard 78
  • Standard 82
  • Standard 88

HCP Prebrief

Today, you will be participating in a whole-class simulation and discussion. The simulation will take place in an athletic training room. Either your instructor or another individual will be a designated facilitator. During this simulation, you will take turns trading roles as a provider or an observer. One student will play the role of head AT and another the role of the other AT. The rest of you will observe the simulation when not in the simulation. The scene for this simulation is baseball athletes before day three of a baseball tournament. Your athletes will enter the athletic training room at random, and you will manage their rehabilitation and other needs. Periodically, the facilitator will pause the simulation for a whole-group discussion and rotate new students into the roles of head and the other AT.

In your presimulation activities, you may have each created a list of five conduct policies you would have for your athletic training room. As a group, we will discuss those policies and vote on the five individual policies you will use during this simulated athletic training room.

After the simulation, you will have the opportunity to self-assess and reflect upon your performance.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

For this simulation, we recommend one of the three-phase debriefing models or the Reflective Practice in Health Care method for your debriefing session.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

You may choose to have this simulation be individually graded, and rather than whole-group participation, you could schedule students in groups of two to interact privately with the simulation scenarios.

While we scheduled approximate time frames for when you should stop during the simulation to discuss and switch groups, you may stop at will when the situation starts getting out of hand (students are not able to control athletes and keep order in their athletic training room).

During your classroom discussion, the students should identify ways of managing the various distractions occurring during the simulation. Some ideas for managing distractions include the following: Ask one athlete to wait 10 minutes to be taped or begin rehabilitation, separate the two athletes who are disrupting the environment and not performing their exercises properly, and remind athletes of the conduct rules of the simulated athletic training room.
