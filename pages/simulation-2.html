Simulation 2

Exercise Illness

Instructor Simulation Summary

An athlete is waiting to see the health care provider (HCP).

Instructor-Only Information

Compared to some other simulations, this is a more complex medical case based on similar cases in the literature. For this simulation, a proctor is not present in the room. Instead, inform the students that all of their decisions and actions need to be reflected in their patient care report (PCR). A list of criteria (rubric) is included that can be used to evaluate the PCR and the student. Consider using the Core AT documentation portal or any patient documentation system that you use to enter patient information. Alternatively, documentation can be turned in on paper or created in a learning management system (LMS). The health care provider (HCP) should be able to obtain a thorough history and ask questions related to syncope or exertion (cardiac, sickle cell, heat syncope, blood sugar problems, blood pressure regulation, neurologic or head injury).

Scenario Synopsis

An athlete recently “passed out.” This patient hit their head, sprained their wrist, and is experiencing wrist pain. The patient has come to discuss this with you.

Content Area

The HCP should be able to take a thorough history and be familiar with wrist injuries and syncope-related conditions.

Learning Objectives

  1. Complete a thorough history and assessment
  2. Create a differential diagnosis list that correlates with the systems involved in a patient response post-exercise
  3. Communicate to patient the plan for participation
  4. Communicate to patient the plan or need for additional care
  5. Provide written documentation of the appointment that illustrates a thorough medical assessment, plan, and restrictions

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 70
  • Standard 71
  • Standard 79
  • Standard 83
  • Standard 89

HCP Prebrief

You will be alone with the patient for your assessment. No proctor will be present. However, it may be videotaped.

Assess and treat all conditions found. Your task is to take a thorough history.

You will document your findings in a patient care report.

Your documentation will indicate if the exam and decisions were appropriate and thorough. You may take notes during the exam.

Supplies have been provided for you, but if you need a supply that is not present, please verbalize this to your proctor, and it will be made available to you (verbally or actually).

You will have no more than 12 minutes to complete this patient examination. You will be given additional time for documentation.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing method from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

This simulation is designed to be a one-on-one encounter without a proctor in the room. However, a proctor could be in the room, and the rubric could be used in place of postsimulation documentation in a PCR.

For safety or to be sure you have an accurate record of the interaction, you can consider an audio or video recording of the event.

The Whyte reference does not appear in the student guide. It may be of interest after the simulation or a good point of discussion for the debrief.
