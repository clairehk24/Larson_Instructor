Simulation 29

A Day in the Life: Juggling Evaluations

Instructor Simulation Summary

Health care providers (HCPs) will evaluate various patients while managing patients’ concerns and other potential ethical dilemmas.

Instructor-Only Information

This simulation relies on students having completed Presimulation Activity 4: Referral Network. Please assign this activity to your students and ask them to bring their completed list to the simulation day. This simulation requires a facilitator. Typically, the instructor serves as the simulation facilitator, but you may also employ another faculty member to serve as facilitator.

Finally, this simulation requires coordination between standardized patients (SPs) and embedded personnel (EPs). It may be helpful for you to meet as a group with the SP/EP team so that each person understands who they will interact with during the simulation.

Scenario Synopsis

This simulation is set in a collegiate athletic training facility. It is meant to resemble a typical day in which athletic trainers (ATs) conduct evaluations and consultations for several patients back-to-back. HCPs will work in pairs for this simulation. This will be a tabletop-style simulation where the simulation facilitators will pause and discuss events at different points during the simulation and provide opportunities for HCP interactions.

This simulation will last 50 to 70 minutes, and all students will observe the simulation when not playing the role of head AT or the other AT.’

Content Area

Students should be able to perform musculoskeletal evaluations and educate a patient on their diagnosis, as well as provide taping to support various pathologies. Students should be knowledgeable in the management of ethical dilemmas.

Learning Objectives

  1. Conduct a thorough musculoskeletal evaluation
  2. Communicate with patients to explain a diagnosis and immediate care of that injury
  3. Promote a patient-provider rapport through opening lines of communication
  4. Identify and address ethical concerns

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 59
  • Standard 65
  • Standard 70
  • Standard 71
  • Standard 76
  • Standard 78
  • Standard 88

HCP Prebrief

Today, you will be participating in a whole-class simulation and discussion. The simulation will take place in an athletic training room. Either your instructor or another individual will be a designated facilitator. During this simulation, you will take turns subbing in and out. We will have one student playing the role of the head AT and another playing the role of the other AT. The rest of you will observe the simulation when not in the simulation. The scene for this simulation is prepractice care for soccer athletes, the day after an away game in an athletic training room. Your athletes will enter the athletic training room at random, and you will manage their health care needs. Periodically, we will pause the simulation for a whole-group discussion and rotate new students into the roles of head and other AT.

In your presimulation activities, there was an activity to create a referral network of physicians and health care professionals in the area, as well as facilities you would refer patients to for acute care. If you completed this assignment, please bring it to the simulation. If it was not assigned, I will lead this discussion with some prior selected choices. As a group, we will discuss those referral networks now and decide on the top five physicians or health care professionals who will be in our referral network and the top facility choice for referring acute care conditions.

After the simulation, you will have the opportunity to self-assess and reflect upon your performance.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to the instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

As this is a tabletop simulation, we recommend one of the three-phase debriefing models or the Reflective Practice in Health Care method for your debriefing session.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

You can reduce the SP requirement by having some patients repeat or even having students play the roles of SPs. The students will need advanced time to prepare and will need to keep things confidential. This may be another learning opportunity for a student to understand a musculoskeletal condition from a patient’s perspective.

You may choose to have this simulation be individually graded, and rather than whole-group participation, you could schedule students in groups of two to interact privately with this simulation scenario.

While approximate time frames have been scheduled for pauses during the simulation to discuss and switch groups, stop at will when the situation starts getting out of hand (students are not able to control athletes and keep order in their athletic training room or there is a risk of patient harm) or for important discussions and observations.

During your classroom discussion, the students should identify ways of managing the multiple patients entering the room during the simulation. Some ideas for managing the patients are as follows: ask one athlete to wait 10 minutes to be taped or begin a lower-stakes evaluation, schedule a parent phone call for a later time, remind athletes of the athlete-AT professional conduct policy.
