Simulation 27

A Day in the Life: General Medical Checks

Instructor Simulation Summary

Health care providers (HCPs) will learn to effectively recognize red flags for a general medical condition and identify where to refer a patient.

Instructor-Only Information

This simulation relies on students having completed Presimulation Activity 2: Referral Network. Please assign this activity to your students and ask them to bring their completed list to the simulation day. This simulation requires a facilitator. Typically, the instructor serves as the simulation facilitator, but you may also employ another faculty member to serve as facilitator. Finally, this simulation requires coordination between standardized patients (SPs) and embedded personnel (EPs). It may be helpful for you to meet as a group with the SP/EP team so that each person understands who they will interact with during the simulation.

Scenario Synopsis

This simulation is set in a collegiate athletic training facility. It is meant to resemble a general medical check day where athletes come in for a medical condition, the athletic trainer (AT) conducts an initial evaluation, and then refers them to the appropriate practitioner. HCPs will work in pairs for this simulation. This will be a tabletop-style simulation where the simulation facilitators will pause and discuss events at different points during the simulation and provide opportunities for HCP interactions. All students will observe the simulation when not playing the role of head AT or other AT.

Content Area

Students should be able to evaluate multiple organ systems using medical evaluation skills and knowledge, educate a patient on the care plan and referral process, and refer when necessary.

Learning Objectives

  1. Conduct a thorough general medical evaluation
  2. Communicate and explain red flags and referral rationale
  3. Promote a patient-provider rapport by opening lines of communication
  4. Develop a care plan for the patient based on the diagnosis

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 59
  • Standard 69
  • Standard 71
  • Standard 88

HCP Prebrief

Today, you will be participating in a whole-class simulation and discussion. Our simulation will take place in the athletic training room. There will be a discussion facilitator for this simulation. During this simulation, you will take turns trading roles as a provider or an observer. We will have one student playing the role of head AT and another playing the role of the other AT. The rest of you will observe the simulation when not in the simulation. The scene for this simulation is an evening general medical check clinic with you and your team physician. Your athletes will enter the athletic training room at random, and you will manage their evaluation and referral needs. Periodically, we will pause the simulation for a whole-group discussion and rotate new students into the roles of head and other AT.

In your presimulation activities, you should have created a referral network of health care professionals you would refer to for medical conditions. You will draw from your referral networks in this simulation because the physician may ask you for a list of locations to refer these athletes.

During and after the simulation, you will have the opportunity to self-assess and reflect upon your performance.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

This simulation involves some sensitive topics involving mental health. If the simulation elicits psychological stress and emotions among your students, we recommend the Trauma-informed Psychologically Safe Debriefing Framework (TiPS) or the PEARLS debriefing framework if the simulation does not elicit psychological stress and emotions.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

You may choose to have this simulation be individually graded, and rather than whole-group participation, you could schedule students in groups of two to interact privately with this simulation scenario.

While we scheduled approximate time frames for when you should stop during the simulation to discuss and switch groups, you may stop at will when the situation starts getting out of hand (students are not able to control athletes and keep order in their athletic training room, or there is a risk of patient harm).
