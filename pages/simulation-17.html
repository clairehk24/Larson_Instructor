Simulation 17

Sensitivity Specificity Insurance Meniscus Simulation (SSIMS)

Instructor Simulation Summary

This simulation is done as a group or small groups as part of a tabletop in-class simulation. The simulation includes activities and instructions on meniscus evaluation, sensitivity, specificity, and discussions about equity and insurance. This is an interactive simulation, but no standardized patient (SP) is present during the simulation.

Instructor-Only Information

This is a tabletop-style simulation that uses slides. It is coordinated like a solve-the-mystery simulation. There is no SP or other personnel required during this simulation other than the instructor. During the simulation, a slide will be shown to the students to prompt a discussion or participation in activities.

During this simulation, issues of race, economics, gender, politics, and many other issues may arise or occur. It is expected that all nonharmful views will be heard, expressed, and discussed by the students and the instructor. In our experience, the students are willing to express and discuss divergent, confusing, or controversial issues without any problems and with respect. It may be important to remind students of the National Athletic Trainers’ Association (NATA) code of conduct, ethics, and the role of health care providers to provide care to their patients. PPT slides have been provided for you to use in leading the simulation.

Scenario Synopsis

As a class divided into small groups, health care providers (HCPs) will evaluate a high school athlete with a potential meniscus injury. This may involve evaluation and rehabilitation of meniscus injury, activities related to special tests, insurance coverage, and discussions with the parent of a minor regarding an injury. This will occur in a high school clinical setting.

Content Area

This simulation applies to students with or without previous training and knowledge of administrative tasks such as insurance, meniscus injuries, sensitivity, specificity, and positive and negative predictive values.

Learning Objectives

  1. Discuss and evaluate the perspective or reasons that individuals may be uninsured or underinsured
  2. Practice and understand the process, barriers, and facilitators of obtaining insurance coverage, including eligibility requirements
  3. Discuss local noninsurance options for athlete injuries
  4. Evaluate the knee for meniscus injury, including the performance of special tests
  5. Discuss, evaluate, calculate, manipulate, and apply the sensitivity and specificity of meniscus tests
  6. Practice a wait-and-see approach by developing parameters and discussing consequences for a potential meniscus injury
  7. Discuss and weigh the role that athletic trainers may play in health care equity and consider individual responsibility to address issues of equity, equality, and value in health care

Debrief

No debrief is included for this simulation because debriefing and discussion occur during the simulation. If students have concerns or seem unsettled at the end, consider a 15-minute discussion aimed at wrapping up the day’s experience by sharing clinical takeaways.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

This simulation involves topics that may be difficult for students to discuss openly and safely. These conversations are important but may be challenging and require respect and civility.
