Simulation 24

Rotator Cuff Complication

Instructor Simulation Summary

Students will be able to recognize signs and symptoms of a medical emergency during a routine postsurgical follow-up examination via a telehealth platform.

Instructor-Only Information

In this simulation, the standardized patient (SP) is usually the proctor as well. These can be different people, but if you choose to do so, the proctor will need to record the interaction or be present during the telehealth meeting or find some other way to measure health care provider (HCP) success (e.g., requiring and reviewing documentation of the event). If the proctor and SP are separate roles, it will require the proctor to be in the room with the HCP to adequately score the individual. This will also allow the proctor to facilitate questions or technical problems, if needed.

Also, students will not receive the rubric in advance of this simulation, to not give away the specific postsurgical complications.

Scenario Synopsis

This is an outpatient surgical clinic follow-up simulation. The health care provider (HCP) will meet with a 53-year-old via telehealth (video platform). The patient previously experienced a right rotator cuff tear repair. The HCPs will speak with the patient six days postoperatively for a follow-up telehealth visit. Patient is currently complaining of right hand swelling and trouble breathing, “like an elephant is on my chest.”

Content Area

Students should have advanced knowledge and skills with patient communication, recognize red flags (particularly respiratory, cardiac, or postsurgical), and postoperative rehabilitation follow-up visits.

Learning Objectives

  1. Interview a patient to obtain their pertinent medical history
  2. Identify signs and symptoms of a medical emergency
  3. Implement referral procedures for patients to report to the surgical center
  4. Provide appropriate management or treatment recommendations to the patient

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 59
  • Standard 70
  • Standard 71

HCP Prebrief

The goal of this brief is to set the ground rules and expectations and explain the format of the simulation.

This is a safe learning environment where you are free to make mistakes.

This will be a telehealth evaluation. The encounter will not be recorded. Instead, please take notes to inform your postsimulation reflection.

Please be sure you have communicated and scheduled your meeting and have appropriate telehealth technology prepared and ready.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

A three-phase debriefing model, such as the Gather, Analyze, Summarize (GAS), may be useful in your debriefing session following this simulation. However, since this simulation is a surprise and involves a life-threatening surgical complication, we understand the simulation may evoke psychological or emotional stress to the students. If that is the case, we recommend following the Trauma-informed Psychologically Safe (TiPS) debriefing framework.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

This simulation could be altered to involve a face-to-face visit rather than a virtual follow-up visit. It could also be an emergency room encounter.

This simulation could be recorded with the proctor out of the room, and then the proctor will score from the video after the encounters.

Given the urgency of treating a pulmonary embolism, we would consider this simulation to be a “critical repeat” for students if they do not refer the SP directly to the emergency room or their surgeon to rule out a pulmonary embolism. “Critical repeat” indicates the need for students to remediate with the instructor and then repeat the simulation.
