Simulation 15

Primary Assessment Simulation Series (PASS)

Instructor Simulation Summary

This simulation involves numerous cases that use the health care provider’s (HCP’s) primary assessment skills. Each situation is time-limited because the primary assessment is time-limited.

Instructor-Only Information

This simulation has four situations available for use. The goal of each simulation is to quickly determine a life or limb threat and to appropriately manage or treat that threat before moving on to other parts of the assessment. Obtaining a good primary assessment is essential for athletic trainers (ATs) to confidently, quickly, and appropriately manage a life or limb threat. Additionally, the primary assessment actions and their order should change depending on the situation. There is a separate rubric for each situation, and these are not in the student guide so as not to give away what students should do in the simulation. See Simulation Variations and Author Notes for two variations that do not require standardized patients (SPs).

Scenario Synopsis

The goal of each situation in this simulation is to quickly determine a life or limb threat and to appropriately manage or treat it before moving on to other parts of the assessment. Obtaining a good primary assessment is essential for athletic trainers (ATs) to confidently, quickly, and appropriately manage a life or limb threat. A good rule of thumb is that a primary assessment is completed in three minutes or less, unless attending to a life or limb threat. Each primary assessment may be unique because the actions and their order should change depending on the situation. There will be multiple stations for this simulation. Depending on the situation, the HCP can ask for a bystander or other provider to assist with some aspect of the assessment or management.

Content Area

HCPs must be able to perform a primary assessment, preferably in three minutes or less, unless managing a life or limb threat. The primary assessment includes the general impression, chief complaint, XABCDE mnemonic (exsanguination, airway, breathing, circulation, disability, exposure), and body system selection. This should lead to a possible differential diagnosis and future actions or plans. An HCP should be able to assess, treat, or identify all life or limb threats within three minutes and understand the body systems affected. At a minimum, the HCP can verbalize actions or needs, and at best, they can perform them.

Learning Objectives

  1. Complete a primary assessment that includes consideration of or actions related to the chief complaint, general impression, XABCDE, body system identification, and future actions
  2. Identify and begin management of all life or limb threats
  3. Appropriately modify the primary assessment based on the patient presentation (e.g., change from ABC to CAB)
  4. Incorporate required tests, evaluations, or other management strategies into the primary assessment as needed
  5. Do not move beyond the primary assessment until all life or limb threats have been found or managed
  6. Able to justify how each action was related to assessing, locating, or managing a life or limb-threat
  7. Unless managing a life or limb threat, the primary assessment should identify life or limb threats within three minutes

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 70
  • Standard 71
  • Standard 76

HCP Prebrief

Each station will last no more than five minutes.

Assess and treat all life or limb threats found.

Supplies have been provided for you, but if you need a supply that is not present, please verbalize this to your proctor, and it will be made available to you (verbally or actually).

The proctor may ask clarifying questions. Being asked questions does not say anything about your performance.

You will see three patients today. Please see the schedule for more details.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

Please be sure to restore and replace any equipment used and after restoring to its original condition, please return your equipment to the starting location.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor prior.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

It can be more educational to put the students in small groups and require each group to make up the case and rubric. On the day of the simulation, a few groups will go first and all students will visit those groups. Then, it will transition, and the other groups will lead and all the students will visit them. Students really enjoy seeing the ideas, processes, and skills of their peers and find it very educational to create and to watch and grade their colleagues. Creating the case requires them to think through what they expect to happen, how it will happen, and to prepare and practice this prior to the simulation day. I will usually suggest the use of a situation they are familiar with or have experienced or to find a case study. It is also great practice to do this simulation as is, but make it an assignment later in the semester to create the cases. They will be amazed at their progress and thoughtfulness.

If personnel is an issue, there are many variations. You can use the variation mentioned earlier. You can simply choose to do one or two situations. You can have one SP be the patient for all students and then switch to a new case. In this way, one SP can do two cases. You could use some students as patients for a couple of cases. Then, at some point switch and use a other students for the remaining cases.
