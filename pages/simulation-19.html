Simulation 19

Snowboard Accident

Instructor Simulation Summary

Health care providers (HCPs) will work as a team to respond to an emergency injury in a snowboard competition and provide immediate care. This simulation will be video recorded, and students will then self-assess their performance as a team.

Instructor-Only Information

This simulation is designed to be self-assessed by the participating students and, therefore, will be video-recorded. After completing the simulation, the students will evaluate the recording of their performance. The standardized patient (SP) information in this simulation includes a list of vital signs. As the facilitator for this simulation, you should write these vital signs on a whiteboard to show the students during the simulation.

Scenario Synopsis

This scenario simulates an emergency happening during a snowboard competition. The students view a video or an image of the injury before the simulation. After watching the video, students will then enter the room as the team of first responders on the scene of the injury.

Content Area

This simulation utilizes advanced knowledge of emergency conditions and communicating with a health care team to provide immediate care to a patient.

Learning Objectives

  1. Conduct a systematic primary and secondary assessment of a patient with a traumatic snowboarding injury
  2. Apply appropriate spinal motion restriction techniques and equipment to safely manage and transport a patient with a suspected spinal injury, considering the potential complications posed by the snowy environment
  3. Effectively communicate with team members to establish priorities, allocate resources, and apply immediate care for a snowboarder with multiple traumatic injuries
  4. Perform advanced trauma interventions, which may include wound management, splinting, spine boarding, and treatment for shock

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 59
  • Standard 69
  • Standard 70
  • Standard 76

HCP Prebrief

This simulation will involve you responding to a catastrophic injury during a snowboard competition. This will be a team-based simulation, so it is essential to work together as a team to provide optimal care to your patient. After viewing the video or image of the injury, you will enter the simulation area where you will find your SP, directly where they landed after the accident.

Please review the simulation schedule. Please arrive at the preparation location at least 10 minutes prior to your simulation so you can delineate roles with your care team. As a team, you will be called into the simulation room when your SP is ready. You will care for this patient as a team. When you complete this simulation, you will have the opportunity to watch your performance and complete a team self-assessment.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop the actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

Since this is a team-based simulation where learners will self-assess later, we recommend using the GAS (gather, analyze, summarize) debriefing method or the reflective practice in health care method.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

This simulation is designed to be conducted indoors but could be adapted for use outdoors in the snow, on a ski slope, or just in the grass. Being outside or in a similar setting provides an important layer of skill and experience to the simulation, and students generally prefer to be in settings that approximate the situation as much as possible. A grassy hill on a cold day would be great, even. This simulation could also be for a skier. You could also implement additional embedded personnel into the simulation by having ski patrol or emergency medical services arrive, and the HCP needs to give them a report.

In the equipment section, we have included items as distractors that the HCPs will not need for this simulation. You may include them or choose not to.

This simulation involves changing vital signs depending on how the HCPs handle the situation. We recommend you have a small, erasable whiteboard to hold up and show the vital signs after the HCPs assess them. This is important in reducing cognitive load. In an actual situation, the vital sign found would not need to be corrected, would likely be written down, and would be easier to track. This whiteboard allows all team members to see and communicate clearly, regardless of what they heard someone say or whether they heard the proctor correct the blood pressure, for instance.

It is not required that you have the students self-assess their management of this incident. You could always score them on the rubric for their outcome with or without the self-assessment.
