Simulation 31

Athlete Orientation Day

Instructor Simulation Summary

In small groups, health care providers (HCPs) will create and facilitate a live presentation for high school athlete orientation day.

Instructor-Only Information

The simulation format includes a 10-minute group presentation delivered as part of an athlete orientation day, where the sports medicine staff educates new athletes, parents, coaches, and other attendees at the start of the season. Students are assigned to groups, and topics are listed in the Simulation Situation section of the student ebook. Presimulation Activity 4 guides the students on the requirements for the presentation and should be completed and submitted in advance of the presentation.

This simulation works best with an instructor (or other facilitator) acting as a moderator in the role of a school administrator. The moderator can select attendees’ questions, guide student responses, respond to questions, guide timing, and help the event run smoothly. This role is optional but highly effective.

Four options for the presentation audience include the following:

Invite individuals familiar with athletics: faculty, graduate students, athletic trainers (ATs), clinical preceptors, and so on. These individuals may offer meaningful insights and relevant questions;

have students adopt roles and ask questions of their peers (they create questions as part of presimulation Activity 4);

use Embedded Personnel (EPs) to portray specific roles; or

any combination of these approaches.

If EPs are utilized, provide them with topic information and ask them to develop two to three questions per topic from the perspectives of different audience members. EP-generated questions tend to be authentic, challenging, and reflective of real-world concerns. After reviewing their submissions, you may also provide the prepared backup questions listed here. Moderators can manage who asks questions, as students are often enthusiastic to participate.

Scenario Synopsis

This simulation involves students, acting as HCPs, giving presentations and then answering questions of parents, athletes, coaches, or any other attendees on topics of interest to this population. Topics may include athletic training services, sudden cardiac arrest, sports nutrition, sport specialization, care for LGBTQ+ athletes, Title IX, immunizations and infectious diseases, heat illnesses, athlete safety and mental health, and head injuries.

Content Area

Students must have an understanding of diverse patient populations and social determinants of health, along with education on preparticipation examinations. It is helpful if students have familiarity with the concepts mentioned earlier, but if not, this is a great way to learn.

Learning Objectives

  1. Explore local high school’s athlete orientation format
  2. Critically appraise a local high school’s athlete orientation format
  3. Create a presentation for an athlete orientation evening
  4. Communicate with parents, coaches, athletes, and other stakeholders on a relevant topic
  5. Answer questions and critically consider topics related to high school athletics (specifically as listed in the description)

HCP Prebrief

During this simulation you will work in groups of health care professionals to deliver preparticipation education to parents, athletes, coaches, and other attendees. This simulation will involve you delivering a presentation followed by questions from the audience. Since this is not a typical simulation, we will not use the phrase “time-out; time-out” to stop the simulation, but we will conduct a debrief after everyone has concluded their presentations. The main audience for the presentation is athletes and their parents but others may attend and participate.

You are free to ask questions of the presenters. These can be from the prepared questions you created when completing Activity 4, or you can spontaneously ask questions. If you are called on to ask a question, please be sure to state your role or perspective. For example, “I am a parent of a [sport] athlete” and then your questions.

If you have any questions about the simulation, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

While many debriefing models would work well for this simulation, given the wide variety of topics students will be presenting, you may find the +Delta debriefing model useful. Alternatively, if you would like students to reflect on their performance as a team, you may wish to utilize the Reflective Practice in Health Care model.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

You may consider having this simulation be an informal session where you invite additional students, local health professionals, or high school athletics staff to attend, and cater lunch while sharing ideas following these presentations. You might also consider bringing in experts in certain areas, such as LGBTQ+ care, sports nutrition, and cardiac care, to facilitate the discussion.
