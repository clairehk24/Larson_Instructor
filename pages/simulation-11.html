Simulation 11

Modalities Shuffle: Cold and Hot

Instructor Simulation Summary

Health care providers (HCPs) will complete three stations with a brief case at each station, requiring the HCP to decide on a superficial hot or cold modality, compression, or manual biofeedback to treat the injury. Students (HCPs) will then set up the modality, answer patient/proctor questions, and then move to the next scheduled location.

Instructor-Only Information

This simulation is designed to have one student play the role of a patient while the other student plays the role of a health care provider (HCP) with one facilitator in the room. Each station should have a video camera to record the encounters so that a proctor (who is also serving as the patient) can score each individual student after the simulation.

If you choose to have the students in pairs as proctor/patient and HCP, you will distribute the same embedded personnel/proctor information to each pair, but be sure you distribute only one patient case to each pair. Students should not know in advance any other pair’s case.

Scenario Synopsis

This simulation is designed to prepare an HCP to provide care to their patients when they have limited access to modality units. Six stations will be set up for this simulation. HCPs will be divided into groups of two and assigned one station for which they must learn the patient case and appropriate modality parameters as a patient/proctor. Students will portray the patient/proctor for each other. The modality options for this simulation are superficial hot and cold (moist hot pack, ice bag, and ice massage), compression, or manual biofeedback via towels. Students will rotate through three of the stations that they are not assigned as patient/proctor. Each station will have a treatment table.

Content Area

Students should have an understanding of superficial hot and cold modalities.

Learning Objectives

  1. Formulate a treatment goal and communicate it to the patient
  2. Select appropriate modalities to treat the patients’ problems
  3. Select appropriate modality parameters (time and application) that align with the treatment goal
  4. Correctly apply the modality, including checking for contraindications, educating, and preparing the patient
  5. Justify each modality selection through answering clarifying questions
  6. Document each treatment in an electronic medical record

CAATE 2020 Standards

  • Standard 64
  • Standard 69
  • Standard 73

HCP Prebrief

Today, you will be participating in a modality unit skills check. This skills check is unique in that you only have access to superficial hot and cold modalities (moist hot pack, ice bag, and ice cup), towels, and compression wraps. This simulation is meant to resemble an athletic training environment where you do not have a budget large enough to purchase and maintain electrical modality units; thus, you will need to get creative with what you do have.

During the simulation, one of you will visit the other stations as an HCP, while the other student serves as the SP and proctor for your station. Halfway through the simulation, you will trade with your peer, and then visit three assigned stations as the HCP. This will allow you to participate as the HCP three times and as the patient/proctor three times. At each station, you will be given an opening statement about the nature of the injury, and then you must determine which modality to perform, explain the treatment goal and the treatment to the patient, and prepare them for the treatment. You will be evaluated on your selection of the modality, your patient communication, and your parameter settings. Please justify your modality choice as the best modality to meet the patients’ needs, indications, and contraindications. You may select a different modality than the one expected, and that is OK as long as you have appropriately justified and selected a safe modality for the condition.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

This simulation involves multiple scenarios with brief opportunities for clinical decision making. As such, the Diamond debriefing method may be useful to articulate and address any clinical and application-based questions.

You may also consider the following prompts to facilitate your debriefing session:

Describe how you arrived at your decisions for each modality choice.

How did the description of the injury help you determine a modality choice?

How confident did you feel in your parameter selection for each modality?

What areas of improvement do you see that need to be addressed in your own performance?

Why are professional communication and demeanor necessary even in a routine modality treatment?

Describe your feelings about performing modalities on and being rated by your peer. How did you feel about rating your peer’s performance?

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

This simulation could be modified to include any modalities students learn in their therapeutic modality courses (except electrical modalities; a separate simulation is available for electric modalities). You may also choose to have students work in pairs and alternate who is the HCP and patient, and then each complete three or more of the prescribed stations.

When having students serve as patients, it is important to give extra attention to other details so that students don’t slip into familiarity and treat the situation as any other day in class. In previous experiences, when the simulation does not create unique or unfamiliar circumstances, the patients complain about HCPs not taking the case seriously or HCPs talking directly to the facilitator, not the patients. When using students as patients, try to do this simulation in a new space (as opposed to the classroom); be sure the patients are dressed in a manner that resembles their case; and consider them having different names, sports, institutions, and so on.

This simulation can be done with actual SPs and proctors, as opposed to students, for each case. If this format is chosen, one SP and one proctor per case would still be the preferred method for conducting the simulation.

If you are interested in a simulation asking students to select from electrical and ultrasound modalities, see Simulation 12, Modalities Shuffle: Electrotherapy and Ultrasound. That simulation also displays students working in pairs and includes a different variation of the schedule, so you may choose either schedule for either simulation.

If you have more than 12 students and wish to add an additional station, following is information you could choose to include in a seventh station.

Optional Station 7 Information

Read to HCP: “I am a cross-country athlete coming in after practice, complaining about my right hamstring contracting nonstop, and it is very painful. About seven months ago, I had a grade 2 hamstring strain or injury during the off-season. The pain was an 8/10 when I strained it. I’ve never had muscle spasms before, but they keep happening; mostly when I activate my hamstrings against resistance. It often happens when rolling over in bed, pulling my heel toward my body, or when tired. I have an abnormal gait and am unable to completely straighten my right leg. I’m tender to palpation along my right hamstring and gastrocnemius. What available modalities or therapies will be helpful to stop or reduce the contraction or spasm?”

Following are the parameters the HCP should select for this case, with the primary treatment goal of relieving pain and reducing or stopping the contraction or spasm.

PNF, Heat Pad, Hydration, Medication Example

Agonist or antagonist contractions

Heat pad for relaxation

Stretching

Recommend hydration and consider medication for pain

Modality Option (Moist Heat Pack) Example

Temperature: 160 to 170 °F (71-76.7 °C) (stored in hydrocollator)

Layers: Six to eight towel layers between pack and skin

Duration: 15 to 20 minutes

Goal: Increase tissue temperature to promote relaxation, improve blood flow, and reduce spasm

What Are the Physiological Effects?

Heat increases blood flow, decreases muscle spindle sensitivity, and promotes relaxation of muscle fibers.
