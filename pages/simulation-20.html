Simulation 20

Head-to-Head (Head Injury Part 1)

Instructor Simulation Summary

This simulation involves a head-to-head collision of two players during a soccer practice with two health care providers (HCPs) providing medical coverage for the practice.

Instructor-Only Information

The simulation of a head-to-head collision of two players during a soccer practice includes bleeding, vomiting, and the need for additional care. One player is hit forcefully in the jaw (vomiting) and the other in the nose and bites their tongue (bleeding). Each patient has their own progression of symptoms, and two future simulations are built from this initial encounter. This simulation can be combined with Simulation 21 (Head Injury Part 2) and Simulation 22 (Head Injury Part 3), which involve a sport concussion assessment tool (SCAT6) assessment and referral to a physical therapist, respectively. This simulation involves the initial on-field assessment only.

Both the standardized patients (SPs) have the same name: Rain Shoemaker.

Tip: This simulation requires that students document the patient injury using a patient care record (PCR) or electronic medical record (EMR). Alternatively, you can create your documentation requirements via paper or other electronic resources. When documenting, students are directed to put the patient’s first name (in this scenario: Rain), then the student’s full name (as the middle name), and then the patient’s last name (Shoemaker). This format is required because students using the same EMR will need to prevent duplicate patient names, this creates a unique name (e.g., as opposed to 17 Rain Shoemakers). This also prevents later difficulties if students participate in Head Injury Part 2 and Part 3. To reduce confusion when talking about Rain Shoemaker, use Rain’s nickname. The Rain with a nosebleed is nicknamed B and the Rain that vomits is nicknamed Skittle. These are their preferred names.

Scenario Synopsis

Two players collide while going for the ball during practice. Immediate on-field assessment and management are required for patient care. The two HCPs must decide how to manage and care for the two patients. One HCP portrays the role of the more experienced or senior provider.

Content Area

Students should have content knowledge in head injuries; concussion; and spinal injury assessment, management, and treatment. History taking for these conditions is necessary. Students should have the ability to communicate and delegate or triage patients.

Learning Objectives

  1. Perform an assessment to rule in or rule out emergent head injury or other emergent conditions; these may include serious head injury, concussion, spinal injury, and other trauma related to a collision (e.g., facial)
  2. Triage based on multiple patients or injuries
  3. Act on return to participation decisions for athletes
  4. Refer patients to additional care as necessary
  5. Coordinate activities as a team to provide the best patient care possible

HCP Prebrief

You will have up to eight minutes to complete the simulation, and the proctor may ask clarifying questions for up to two minutes.

Treat this person as a real patient. Provide all necessary evaluations and treatments as you would in real life.

You will start on the sideline. You just saw the incident as described in the situation information and with the pictures.

If you have any questions, please contact your instructor in advance.

If there is a piece of equipment you need that is not available, please verbalize this equipment, and it will be made available to you, or you will be instructed to verbalize its use.

Please remember the fiction contract and treat this situation as a real situation.

SAFETY: If any person says “Time-out; time-out,” please stop actions immediately and as safely as possible. Unless you can see that the SP needs assistance, you should immediately leave the area and find a proctor.

If you have any questions, please speak to your instructor.

Debrief

Please select one of the provided debriefing methods from the Introduction. It is important to have a whole-class debriefing session following each simulation.

Since students are working together as a team, this simulation may benefit from utilizing the Reflective Practice in Health Care debriefing model.

Simulation Variations and Author Notes

This simulation is written for two SPs and two HCPs. However, this simulation could be done with one SP and one HCP, if preferred. To do this, simply choose which patient role you want the SP to memorize and inform the students that only one patient will be present for the simulation.

It is easiest to combine Simulations 20 and 21 on the same day. To do this, after the student evaluates a head injury, Simulation 20, they are provided the prebrief from Simulation 21 while moving to a new space to complete a SCAT6. During this time, the same patient can go to the new space. The same proctor can either read the Simulation 21 prebrief and accompany the student to the new space, or a new proctor can do this. Doing this immediately may reduce confusion; keep a more continuous time line; and ensure the student sees the same patient, same SP, and completes paperwork all at once.

If time does not allow these to be conducted as a pair, the HCP can see the same patient (not necessarily the same SP) they saw in the previous head-to-head simulation, Simulation 20 (Head Injury Part 1). Be sure to discuss and practice this when training the SPs. On the simulation day, be sure your schedule indicates which patient the HCP saw during the head-to-head simulation. During the rehearsal and the day of the simulation, simply give the SP a uniquely colored note card that lists the patient’s name, nickname, and symptoms (B = Nosebleed, tongue bleed, temple pain, went home or Skittle = Vomit, hospital, negative scan or head injury, went home). Then, when the HCP asks questions, they will have prompts and notes to use and will know which patient they are with and which HCP is present. If note cards B and Skittle are of different colors, then without reading the note card, the SP and the proctor will know which patient they are portraying for that situation.
